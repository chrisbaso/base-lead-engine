A retirement income score is a simple way to turn a complicated question into a useful first signal. The question is not just how much money is in the account. The question is how much monthly income that money can reasonably support when combined with Social Security and other dependable income sources.

RetireReadyMN uses a simplified baseline. It estimates portfolio income using a 4% withdrawal benchmark and adds your estimated monthly Social Security benefit. That combined number is compared with the monthly retirement income you said you want in today's dollars. The score is then grouped into Red, Yellow, or Green.

Green generally means the baseline income appears close to or above the desired income. It does not mean the plan is finished. Taxes, inflation, healthcare costs, sequence risk, and longevity can still change the answer. A Green score means the starting math is encouraging.

Yellow generally means the baseline shows a manageable gap. The gap may improve through Social Security timing, retirement age changes, savings changes, tax strategy, or a more detailed income plan. Yellow is often the most useful result because it shows where planning can make a measurable difference.

Red generally means the baseline income is meaningfully below the desired income. That does not mean retirement is impossible. It means the plan needs more detail. Spending goals, work timeline, guaranteed income, investment risk, housing decisions, and tax planning may all need to be reviewed together.

The score is intentionally educational. It does not recommend investments, annuities, insurance, or any specific product. It also does not promise that a withdrawal rate will work in every market. A real plan would look at account types, pension income, debt, cash reserves, health insurance, household taxes, and what happens if one spouse outlives the other.

The value of the score is direction. If the number is strong, you know what to protect. If the number is weak, you know what to investigate. If it is in the middle, you know which tradeoffs deserve attention before retirement decisions become permanent.
