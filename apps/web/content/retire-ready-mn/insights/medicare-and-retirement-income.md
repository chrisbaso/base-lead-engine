Medicare is a major milestone, but it does not make healthcare free. Retirees may still pay premiums, deductibles, copays, prescription costs, supplemental coverage, dental and vision expenses, and costs that are not covered. A retirement income plan should include healthcare as a recurring budget category, not an afterthought.

Income can affect Medicare costs. Higher-income retirees may pay income-related monthly adjustment amounts. The calculation uses income from a prior tax year, which means today's Roth conversion, capital gain, or large IRA withdrawal can affect future premiums. That does not mean those moves are wrong. It means the premium effect should be part of the analysis.

Healthcare spending also changes over time. Early retirement years may include more travel and discretionary spending. Later years may include higher medical costs or care needs. A flat spending estimate can miss the way real retirement expenses shift. Some plans use different spending phases to reflect that reality.

Prescription drug planning deserves attention. Formularies, pharmacy networks, and drug needs can change. Reviewing coverage annually can be valuable, especially for households with recurring prescriptions. This is not investment advice, but it is part of protecting retirement cash flow.

Long-term care is a separate risk. Medicare does not cover every long-term care need. Some households self-fund the risk. Some use insurance. Some rely on family support. Avoiding the topic does not make it disappear. The right approach depends on assets, health, family, and preferences.

Planning prompts include:

- Estimate recurring Medicare-related premiums
- Review income thresholds before large taxable moves
- Include prescriptions and supplemental coverage
- Revisit coverage annually
- Separate normal healthcare from long-term care risk
- Consider the surviving spouse's healthcare budget

Healthcare planning is not just about choosing coverage. It is about making sure the retirement paycheck can absorb costs without forcing avoidable tax or investment decisions.
