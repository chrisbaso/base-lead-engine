Annuities are often discussed in extremes. Some people treat them as the answer to every retirement income problem. Others dismiss them entirely. A more useful approach is to start with the job that needs to be done. If a household has an income gap, a longevity concern, or a need to protect essential spending, guaranteed income may deserve analysis. That does not mean every annuity is appropriate.

The first question is income need. How much monthly income must be dependable? Social Security and pensions may already cover a large portion. If they do not, an annuity can be compared with portfolio withdrawals, bond ladders, cash reserves, and spending adjustments. The product should compete against alternatives.

The second question is liquidity. Money committed to an annuity may not be easily available for emergencies, opportunities, or changing plans. Some contracts offer access features, but they vary. A retiree should understand what money remains flexible after any purchase.

The third question is inflation. A fixed payment can feel strong at the start and weaker later if prices rise. Some products include inflation features, but those features have costs or tradeoffs. A retirement income plan should identify which expenses are fixed, which rise with inflation, and which can adjust.

The fourth question is complexity. Indexed strategies, riders, surrender schedules, caps, spreads, participation rates, and income bases can be confusing. A household should understand the actual guaranteed result, the non-guaranteed upside, and the cost of each feature.

Before considering a product, ask:

- What income problem is this meant to solve?
- What alternatives were compared?
- How much liquidity remains?
- What fees, surrender charges, or limits apply?
- What is guaranteed and what is only illustrated?
- How does this affect the surviving spouse?

Annuities can be useful tools when they earn a clear role in the plan. They can also be oversold. The starting point should be the income gap and the household's priorities, not a product brochure.
